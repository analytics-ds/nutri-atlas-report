## How the immune system really works

The immune system is a complex network of cells and organs directly influenced by nutrition, sleep and stress levels. A deficiency in certain micronutrients can measurably weaken the immune response.

### The three key micronutrients

Vitamin C, vitamin D and zinc are considered the most researched nutrients in relation to immune function.

## Vitamin C in detail

Vitamin C supports numerous immune cell functions and acts as an antioxidant.

### Proven effects

- Shortens the duration of colds by an average of 8% in adults
- Supports white blood cell function
- Acts as an antioxidant against oxidative stress

## Vitamin D and zinc compared

| Nutrient | Main source | Recommended daily intake |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin D | Sunlight, fatty fish | 20 µg (800 IU) |
| Zinc | Meat, legumes, nuts | 7-10 mg |
| Vitamin C | Citrus fruit, bell peppers | 95-110 mg |

### A frequently cited body of research

> "Vitamin D deficiency is associated with increased susceptibility to respiratory infections, particularly during winter months."
> — Nutrients, systematic review

## Practical steps for daily life

1. Have vitamin D levels checked regularly in winter
2. Include citrus fruit or bell peppers in your diet daily
3. Actively reduce chronic stress through sleep and exercise

A strong immune system is closely linked to good [sleep](/en/blog/sleep-better/), a stable [metabolism](/en/blog/boost-metabolism/), and generally healthy [hair care](/en/blog/hair-loss-in-women/) as a visible sign of wellbeing.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>Which vitamins strengthen the immune system the most?</summary>

Vitamin C, vitamin D and zinc show the strongest scientific evidence for supporting immune function. A meta-analysis shows that regular vitamin C intake can shorten the duration of colds by an average of 8% in adults.

</details>

<details>
<summary>How many people have a vitamin D deficiency?</summary>

Estimates suggest up to 40% of the European population has insufficient vitamin D levels, especially during winter months with less sunlight. Vitamin D plays a central role in regulating the immune response.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Can stress actually weaken the immune system?</summary>

Chronic stress measurably raises cortisol levels, which suppresses white blood cell activity and increases susceptibility to infections. Studies show a measurable link between sustained stress and more frequent colds.

</details>
