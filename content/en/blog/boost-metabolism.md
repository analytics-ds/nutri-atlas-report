> **In brief:**
> 1. One kilogram of muscle mass burns about 13 kcal per day at rest, nearly three times more than fat tissue.
> 2. Sleep deprivation under 6 hours measurably alters metabolism after just one week.
> 3. Basal metabolic rate in women declines by 2-3% per decade starting around age 40.

## What really affects metabolism

Basal metabolic rate accounts for 60-70% of daily energy expenditure for most people. Anyone looking to influence it should focus on muscle mass, nutrition and sleep rather than short-term diet tricks.

### Muscle mass as a key lever

Muscle tissue burns significantly more energy at rest than fat tissue, making strength training one of the most effective long-term measures.

## Nutrition and the thermic effect

Not all nutrients require the same amount of energy to digest, an effect known as the thermic effect of food.

### Thermic effect by nutrient

- Protein: 20-30% of calories consumed are used for digestion
- Carbohydrates: 5-10%
- Fat: 0-3%

## Sleep, stress and metabolism working together

| Factor | Effect on metabolism | Timeframe |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep deprivation (<6h) | Declining insulin sensitivity | Measurable after 1 week |
| Chronic stress | Elevated cortisol, promotes belly fat | Weeks to months |
| Strength training | Increases basal metabolic rate | 8-12 weeks |

### An often underestimated body of research

> "Just one week of shortened sleep is enough to measurably lower insulin sensitivity."
> — Annals of Internal Medicine, sleep research

## Concrete steps for daily life

1. Schedule two to three strength training sessions per week
2. Spread protein-rich meals throughout the day
3. Aim for consistent sleep of at least 7 hours

A healthy metabolism is closely linked to [quality sleep](/en/blog/sleep-better/), a [strong immune system](/en/blog/strengthen-immune-system/), and generally healthy [hair care](/en/blog/hair-loss-in-women/) as a sign of overall wellbeing.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>Can you really boost your metabolism?</summary>

Basal metabolic rate can be influenced to a limited extent, mainly through building muscle: one kilogram of muscle mass burns about 13 kcal per day at rest, compared to about 4.5 kcal for fat tissue. Adequate protein intake and regular strength training are the best-supported levers.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What role does sleep play in metabolism?</summary>

Sleep deprivation measurably lowers insulin sensitivity and raises the hunger hormone ghrelin, making weight management harder. Studies show measurable metabolic changes after just one week of less than 6 hours of sleep.

</details>

<details>
<summary>At what age does metabolism slow down in women?</summary>

Basal metabolic rate in women declines by an average of 2-3% per decade starting around age 40, mainly due to the natural loss of muscle mass. Strength training can specifically counteract this effect.

</details>
